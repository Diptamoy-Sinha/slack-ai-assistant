# Open questions

Gaps the current three sources cannot close. Per [[SCHEMA]], a query the wiki
cannot answer is itself a finding.

## Collection subjects not yet covered

The collection description names subjects that these three items barely touch.
Each is a hole waiting on further ingest.

**Water-power.** Named as a collection subject. Absent from all three sources.
Bush's factory runs on steam — the advertisements offer steam engines, boilers,
dynamos and electric lighting `[BUSH-1897 scan 3]` — with no water wheel or
turbine anywhere. Nothing in Marcet either.

**Extraction of magnesium from seawater.** The 1809 root is here and it is a
direct one: Epsom salt "is obtained from sea-water and from several springs"
`[COC-1809 p.201 (scan 235)]`, and muriatic acid's principal combinations
include magnesia `[COC-1809 scan 254]`. But the metal is not. [[humphry-davy]]
isolated magnesium in 1808, contemporary with this edition, yet the appended
Bakerian Lecture covers only the fixed alkalies. The industrial process —
Dow at Freeport, 1941 — is a century and a half downstream of anything here.
See [[magnesia-and-epsom-salt]].

**Water-hardness and softening.** Hardness is well covered; **softening** is
not. Neither source describes a softening *process*. Bush's remedy is to source
soft water or distil it `[BUSH-1897 p.26 (scan 30)]`. No lime-soda, no base
exchange, no zeolite. Given the 1897 date, this looks like a genuine absence in
the mineral-water trade rather than a gap in the sources — but it wants
confirming. See [[water-hardness-and-softening]].

**Desalination.** Absent entirely, despite [[distillation]] being well
represented as a technique. Nobody in these three sources proposes making
seawater drinkable.

**Material types not yet ingested.** The collection includes advertisements,
museum objects, oral history interviews, and photographs. Only print materials
are in `raw/` so far. The advertisements bound into
[[bush-recipes-aerated-mineral-waters-1897]] are a preview of what a dedicated
advertising ingest would add — they are already the best evidence in the wiki
for the supply chain around a water factory.

## Specific unknowns

**"Salvol"** — Bush's plant disinfectant, used at 10 per cent solution
`[BUSH-1897 p.25 (scan 29)]`. Trade name; composition not given. A phenolic?

**"Biaris"** — Bush's registered artificial mineral water, licensed rather than
published: "For the terms and conditions of permission to manufacture, apply
direct to them" `[BUSH-1897 p.87 (scan 91)]`. Composition unknown. The one
formula in the book Bush would not print.

**"Calcis Chlor."** — first in Bush's list of imitated springs
`[BUSH-1897 p.90 (scan 94)]`, and the only entry that is a chemical name rather
than a place. Chloride of lime? Calcium chloride water? It does not belong in a
list of European spas and may be a compositor's insertion.

**Mr. Usher** — made soda water in New York "for a year or more" before April
1809, with "a good reputation and an extensive sale", sold from a fountain and
in stone bottles `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`. No first name. Predates
Silliman's New York venture. See [[benjamin-silliman]].

**Noyes Darling & Co.** — Silliman's New York partners at the Tontine Coffee
House `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`. Nothing further in the source.

**Why is Ballston absent from Bush's 1897 list?** All ten imitated springs are
European `[BUSH-1897 p.90 (scan 94)]`. Did the American springs never travel, or
did they fade at home? See [[ballston-spa]].

**"Mr. Tennant"** — credited with discovering a limestone containing magnesia
"in very great quantities" `[COC-1809 p.176 (scan 209)]`. Smithson Tennant,
presumably; the reference is undated.

**Dr. De la Rive** — credited in a footnote for the explanation of the singing
hydrogen flame, *Journals of the Royal Institution* vol. i p. 259
`[COC-1809 p.92 (scan 118)]`.

## Dating and provenance

**Silliman's New Haven start.** "About three years ago" from an 1809 imprint
gives ~1806 `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`. Approximate, from the source's own
vagueness.

**FFS-1961 print run.** Issued August 1961 `[FFS-1961 p.2]` but the printing
code at the foot of p.3 reads `620885°—62`, indicating a 1962 impression. The
scanned copy is a reprint of the 1961 text.

**Bush's fifty years.** "An experience with the trade from its infancy — a period
of fifty years" `[BUSH-1897 p.vii (scan 11)]` places the firm's origin around
1847, and the claim that there were fifty makers "sixty years ago"
`[BUSH-1897 p.iv (scan 8)]` points at ~1837. Both are round numbers in a sales
document.

## Method questions

**Bush's four-determinand water analysis** — ammonia, chlorine, nitrates, solid
matter `[BUSH-1897 p.103 (scan 107)]`. What were the actual bench methods and
the pass/fail thresholds behind "our opinion as to fitness for drinking
purposes"? The fee is published; the standard is not. See
[[water-quality-analysis]].

**Marcet's eudiometer.** Listed in the contents under phosphorus
`[COC-1809 contents (scan 8)]` but not read in detail during this ingest. Worth
a pass — it is the one genuine measuring instrument in the book.
