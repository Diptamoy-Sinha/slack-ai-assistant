# Contradictions and corrections

Logged, not silently resolved. Per [[SCHEMA]], a period claim is never rewritten
into a modern one — the disagreement is the record.

## OCR artefacts

**`(1899)` for `(1809)`** — [[conversations-on-chemistry-1809]]
The Account of Mineral Waters reads: "An establishment of the same kind... was
effected in New-York in April of this year, **(1899)** by Noyes Darling & Co."
`[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`
The book was printed in 1809 and the phrase is "this year". A `0` misread as a
`9`. Corrected wherever quoted, marked here. See [[benjamin-silliman]].

**`Harrowgate` vs `Harrogate`** — [[conversations-on-chemistry-1809]]
Contents and index use "Harrowgate"; the body uses "Harrogate"
`[COC-1809 contents (scan 8); p.104 (scan 134); index (scan 416)]`. Both are
period-legitimate spellings of the same place, not OCR error. Wiki uses
Harrogate, with the variant as an alias. See [[harrogate]].

**The `æ` ligature** — [[bush-recipes-aerated-mineral-waters-1897]]
"ærated" appears in the text layer as `erated`, `srated`, `#rated`, `Alrated`,
`seration`, `eration`. Normalised silently throughout.

**Scattered letter substitutions** — `Salphat`→`Sulphat`, `swlphat`→`sulphat`,
`Piltering`→`Filtering`, `Freneh`→`French`, `exhilirating`→`exhilarating`.
Repaired silently.

## Internal inconsistencies within a source

**Medicinal multiplier for soda water** — [[bush-recipes-aerated-mineral-waters-1897]]
Potash, Magnesia and the named-spring waters all say **three times** the
ordinary quantity for medicinal strength. Soda Water alone reads "For Medicinal
Soda Water use **8** times the above quantity" `[BUSH-1897 p.89 (scan 93)]`.
Almost certainly `3` misread as `8` — the surrounding recipes and the general
statement that medicinal waters "should contain three times that quantity"
`[BUSH-1897 p.87 (scan 91)]` both point that way. **Not** silently corrected: a
reader reproducing the recipe should know the printed text is ambiguous. See
[[sodium-bicarbonate]].

**Tartaric acid, recommended and blamed** — [[bush-recipes-aerated-mineral-waters-1897]]
p.19: tartaric is cheaper and "oftener used, but in most cases Citric Acid is
preferable". p.26: the crystalline bottle deposit is tartrates, "chiefly due...
to the action of Tartaric Acid on the lime and other salts in the water"
`[BUSH-1897 pp.19, 26 (scan 23, 30)]`. Bush never connects the two, and his
recipes overwhelmingly specify tartaric. Not a contradiction of fact — a
recommendation that his own diagnosis undercuts. See
[[water-hardness-and-softening]].

**"How to Purify Water" vs the fallout warning** — [[family-food-stockpile-1961]]
The purification section presents boiling, bleach and iodine under an
unqualified heading `[FFS-1961 pp.11–12]`. The warning box on the same spread
says fallout-exposed water from open sources "may be unsafe to drink" and
directs the reader to protected sources instead `[FFS-1961 p.12]`. Nothing in
the layout connects them. None of the three methods removes dissolved
radionuclides, and boiling concentrates them. See [[waterborne-contamination]].

**Fidelity vs improvement** — [[conversations-on-chemistry-1809]]
The artificial waters are "perfectly imitated" from analysis
`[COC-1809 p.354 (scan 392)]`, and simultaneously the artificial Ballstown water
"is however more powerful" at equal volume `[COC-1809 p.355 (scan 393)]`. Both
cannot hold. See [[ballston-spa]].

## Claims superseded by later evidence

**Carbonic acid cannot be liquefied**
Marcet: "No: for, as I told you before, it is a permanent elastic fluid."
`[COC-1809 p.120 (scan 150)]`
Bush, in the same collection: carbonic acid gas "compressed into iron cylinders
is now offered to the trade" `[BUSH-1897 p.19 (scan 23)]`.
Resolved by fact — CO₂ liquefies readily under pressure; Thilorier produced the
solid in 1835. The two sources are 88 years apart and the wiki records both. See
[[carbonic-acid]].

**Flame is always burning hydrogen**
Marcet: "whenever you see flame, you may infer that it is owing to the formation
and burning of hydrogen gas... which, with only one or two apparent exceptions,
does not belong to any other combustible." `[COC-1809 p.93 (scan 119)]`
Wrong — flame is gas-phase combustion of any volatile fuel. Her own hedge shows
she knew the rule was strained. See [[composition-of-water]].

**Evaporation as dissolution in air**
The caloric-solvent account of clouds, rain and dew
`[COC-1809 pp.38–40 (scan 60–62)]` predicts the phenomena and misidentifies the
mechanism. Kept as written, with the modern note on the page. See
[[caloric-theory]] and [[evaporation-and-the-water-cycle]].

**Water storage ration**
1961: ½ gallon per person per day for drinking and food preparation
`[FFS-1961 p.10]`. Current US guidance is 1 gallon. Not an error in 1961 — a
sedentary-shelter minimum, explicitly qualified as such. See [[water-storage]].

**Household bleach at 5.25%**
`[FFS-1961 p.11]`. No longer a safe assumption; modern products vary and many
contain additives. The dosage table should not be used from a current bottle.
See [[sodium-hypochlorite]].
