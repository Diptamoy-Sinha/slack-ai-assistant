# Epsom

Surrey town that gave its name to a salt.

Caroline asks why sulphat of magnesia is called Epsom salt:

> "Because there is a spring in the neighbourhood of Epsom, which contains this
> salt in great abundance." `[COC-1809 p.176 (scan 209)]`

An instance of the general pattern in this collection — a spring's composition
becoming a commodity name, then a commodity, then something manufactured without
reference to the spring at all. The same route [[seltzer-water]] and
[[harrogate]] travelled.

Marcet immediately widens the sources: Epsom salt "is obtained from sea-water
and from several springs, or may be prepared by the direct combination of its
ingredients" `[COC-1809 p.201 (scan 235)]`. The place name survives the place's
irrelevance.

See [[magnesia-and-epsom-salt]] for the chemistry, and
[[water-hardness-and-softening]] for why magnesium sulphate matters to a water
supply.
