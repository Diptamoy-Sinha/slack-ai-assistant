# Ballston Spa, New York

Spelled "Ballstown" throughout the 1809 text. One of the three waters
manufactured artificially in America by 1809, alongside soda and
[[seltzer-water]] `[COC-1809 p.354 (scan 392)]`.

## Reputation

> "The Ballstown water is well known in the United States as a gentle cathartic,
> an active diuretic, a remedy against gravelly complaints, a tonic to the
> stomach, and generally to the system; not to mention its efficacy against
> rheumatic, and cutaneous complaints, when applied externally, as well as
> internally." `[COC-1809 pp.354–355 (scan 392–393)]`

"Gravelly complaints" — urinary calculi.

## The artificial version, and a claim worth pausing on

> "It remains to be added, only, that the artificial Ballstown water is found by
> experience to produce the effects of the natural water; it is however more
> powerful, and therefore an equal quantity produces more marked effects."
> `[COC-1809 p.355 (scan 393)]`

This is the imitation trade's strongest possible claim, made about an American
spring for an American readership: the copy is not merely equivalent but
*better*. It follows directly from Marcet's point that artificial waters "may be
prepared stronger, or weaker, as occasion requires" `[COC-1809 p.121 (scan 151)]`.

It also quietly concedes the thing that makes the claim testable — if the
artificial water is more powerful at equal volume, then the natural spring's
composition was not in fact reproduced. Fidelity and improvement are being
claimed at once. See [[mineral-waters]].

Soda water, by contrast, is judged **preferable to** Ballstown water "as a
palliative, and even a remedy, in some cases of urinary calculi and gravelly
complaints" `[COC-1809 p.355 (scan 393)]` — a formulated product beating a
natural spring on its own indication.

## Commercially

Ballstown water was one of three on tap at the Tontine Coffee House fountains
opened by Noyes Darling & Co. in New York, April 1809, and was "prepared
according to an accurate analysis". See [[benjamin-silliman]].

Bush's 1897 catalogue of imitated springs is entirely European — Ballston does
not appear `[BUSH-1897 p.90 (scan 94)]`. Filed in [[open-questions]].
