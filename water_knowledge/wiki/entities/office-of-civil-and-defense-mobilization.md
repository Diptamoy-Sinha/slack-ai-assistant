# Office of Civil and Defense Mobilization (OCDM)

Co-preparer of [[family-food-stockpile-1961]] with the [[usda]]
`[FFS-1961 p.2]`.

## The two recommended methods

OCDM's guidance frames the whole pamphlet `[FFS-1961 p.2]`:

> "1. Increase your regular food supply so there will always be a 2-week supply
> of food for your family in your home. Replace food as it is used.
>
> 2. Store and maintain in your fallout shelter or home a special 2-week
> stockpile of survival foods. Choose foods that will keep for months without
> refrigeration, require little or no cooking, and yet will provide a reasonably
> well-balanced family diet."

Either, or a combination — "The important thing is that you have enough food in
your home or shelter to last until it is safe and possible to get other foods."
`[FFS-1961 p.2]`

## The policy behind the two weeks

The number is not a recommendation; it is a quoted obligation from the National
Plan for Civil Defense and Defense Mobilization `[FFS-1961 p.3]`:

> "Individuals and families will be prepared to exist on personal stocks of
> survival items in their homes and shelter areas for 2 weeks following attack."

And the responsibility is placed explicitly: "Doing this is an individual and
family responsibility. This responsibility is placed directly on the individual
citizen and family by the National Plan..." `[FFS-1961 p.3]`

Everything quantitative in the water section — 7 gallons for drinking, 7 more
for hygiene — is arithmetic performed on that sentence. See [[water-storage]].

## The threat model

> "An H-bomb explosion can blanket an area many miles from the target area with
> dangerous radioactive fallout. If you and your family survive the explosion,
> you may have difficulty obtaining food and water from regular sources without
> overexposure to fallout radiation. Essential services, such as gas,
> electricity, and water, may be disrupted." `[FFS-1961 p.3]`

Two distinct failure modes for water: the supply stops, and the open sources are
contaminated. The pamphlet's answers are storage for the first and exclusion for
the second. See [[waterborne-contamination]].

Local authorities are expected to return: "As soon as possible after attack — if
there is one — local authorities will inform you of safe sources of food and
water." `[FFS-1961 p.3]` The same assumption underwrites the water section's
deference to "health authorities" for testing `[FFS-1961 p.11]` — see
[[water-quality-analysis]].
