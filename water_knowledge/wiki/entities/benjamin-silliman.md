# Benjamin Silliman

Professor at Yale, and the figure at the centre of the American artificial
mineral water trade as the 1809 appendix describes it.

## The New Haven and New York establishments

> "The manufacture of mineral waters upon correct chemical principles was
> undertaken in New-Haven, Connecticut, about three years ago; and during the
> last summer, a public establishment for this purpose was opened in the same
> town, under the direction of Professor Silliman." `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`

"About three years ago" from an 1809 imprint places the start around 1806.

> "An establishment of the same kind, and under the same direction, was effected
> in New-York in April of this year, (1899) by Noyes Darling & Co. Fountains of
> Ballstown, Soda, and Seltzer waters were opened in the bar of the Tontine
> Coffee House." `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`

The "(1899)" is an OCR misreading of 1809 — logged in [[contradictions]].

## The fountains

The passage is unusually detailed about the fittings, and reads like a
promotional notice:

> "The cisterns are placed in the cellar, and the waters are conveyed into the
> bar in block-tin tubes, which pass up into mahogany pillars, crowned with gilt
> urns, lettered with the names of the respective waters. The pillars, with
> their urns, stand a foot apart, and the middle one is raised above the others;
> silver stop-cocks inserted into the sides of the pillars, give the whole much
> neatness and richness of appearance." `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`

**Block-tin tubes and silver stop-cocks** — non-reactive materials, chosen so
the carbonated water does not attack the plumbing or pick up metal. Bush is
still making the same argument in 1897 about "pure tin, silver plate or other
innocuous metal" `[BUSH-1897 p.14 (scan 18)]`; see [[waterborne-contamination]].

The proprietors planned to open further fountains at the City Hotel in May, "in
a spacious room, fitted up and ornamented in a handsome style, and adapted to
the accommodation of ladies as well as gentlemen" `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`.
The soda fountain as respectable mixed-sex public space, from the outset.

## The competition

The same report names two rivals, which is how we know it was already a trade
and not a novelty:

- **Mr. Usher**, New York — soda water "for a year or more", "a good reputation
  and an extensive sale", sold from a fountain and in stone bottles, and about
  to expand `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`
- **Philadelphia** — "There have been, for some time, manufactories of mineral
  waters in the city of Philadelphia, and we are informed that these waters have
  been extensively used." `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`

Bottling for export is noted: the waters "are bottled for exportation, in any
quantity demanded" `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`.

## The quality claim

[[ballston-spa]] and [[seltzer-water]] "are prepared according to an accurate
analysis; and in order to give the Soda water its proper efficacy, it is made
with the full proportion of Soda directed by the dispensatories."
`[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]` See [[water-quality-analysis]].

appended to the same volume.
