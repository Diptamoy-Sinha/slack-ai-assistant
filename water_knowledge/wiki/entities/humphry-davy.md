# Humphry Davy (1778–1829)

Named on the title page of [[conversations-on-chemistry-1809]]: the edition adds
"Some late Discoveries on the subject of the Fixed Alkalies, by H. Davy, Esq.,
of the Royal Society" `[COC-1809 title page (scan 3)]`.

## The Bakerian Lecture

An abridgement of Davy's Bakerian Lecture "on the decomposition of the fixed
alkalies" occupies pp. 338–351 `[COC-1809 contents (scan 13); scan 374]`. The
collection description records that the isolation of potassium and sodium is
described there.

This is why the 1809 American edition exists in the form it does. Davy's
electrolytic decomposition of potash and soda in 1807 demolished the standing
classification: substances treated as simple bodies turned out to be compounds
of a metal with oxygen. Marcet's own text registers the shock in advance —
Mrs. B. tells the students that "some of those, which we now class among the
simple bodies, may, in fact, be compound; indeed, you will soon find that
discoveries of this kind are by no means unfrequent" `[COC-1809 p.106 (scan 136)]`.

The appended lecture is the discovery she is preparing them for, bound into the
same volume.

## In the appendix text

The Davy material at scans 374–391 discusses naphtha as a medium for handling
the reactive metals — "colourless and recently distilled" naphtha, "double
distilled naphtha", specific gravity 861, and the observation that the metal
"swims in the double distilled naphtha" at 4 °F `[COC-1809 scan 380–381, 386]`.
Potassium reacting with sulphur compounds evolves sulphurated hydrogen
`[COC-1809 scan 384]`. See [[sulphurated-hydrogen]].

## What is not here

Davy also isolated magnesium, calcium, strontium and barium in 1808, but this
edition's appendix is confined to the **fixed alkalies**. Marcet's own treatment
of magnesia and lime stays at the level of the earths, without their metals. See
[[magnesia-and-epsom-salt]], [[lime]], and [[open-questions]].
