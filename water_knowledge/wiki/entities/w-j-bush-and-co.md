# W. J. Bush & Co., Limited

London manufacturer of essences, flavourings and mineral-water salts; publisher
of [[bush-recipes-aerated-mineral-waters-1897]].

**Address:** Artillery Lane, Bishopsgate, London `[BUSH-1897 p.ix (scan 13)]`
**Directors:** [[baron-de-bush]] (Chairman), with James Mortimer Bush and Alfred
Walter Bush as Joint Managing Directors `[BUSH-1897 title page (scan 5)]`

## Claimed history and standing

- **Fifty years in the trade** by 1897: recipes "compiled after the result of an
  experience with the trade from its infancy — a period of fifty years"
  `[BUSH-1897 p.vii (scan 11)]`
- **Thirteen gold medals** and highest awards at exhibitions in England,
  Australia, Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Holland, India "and other parts
  of the world" `[BUSH-1897 p.viii (scan 12)]`
- Their chairman appointed juror at the great exhibitions of London 1884,
  Antwerp 1885, Brussels 1888, Edinburgh 1890 `[BUSH-1897 pp.viii–ix (scan 12–13)]`

## Claimed inventions

The preface is a sustained argument that Bush created much of the modern trade:

- **Soluble Essences** — for making ærated drinks "without rendering them
  turbid", said to impart better taste than essential oils, which "undergo, even
  when pure, resinous and terebinthine decomposition" `[BUSH-1897 p.v (scan 9)]`
- **French Cream Gum Extract** — "for producing a creamy head on ærated
  beverages, thereby giving the various drinks a more tempting appearance"
  `[BUSH-1897 pp.v–vi (scan 9–10)]`
- **Bush's Preservative** — see [[preservatives-and-antiseptics]]
- **"Biaris"** (registered) — "A new artificial mineral water, invented by W. J.
  Bush & Co., Ltd., possessing tonic and refreshing properties. For the terms
  and conditions of permission to manufacture, apply direct to them."
  `[BUSH-1897 p.87 (scan 91)]` A licensed formula, not a recipe.

They also claim to have "invented a very large proportion of existing beverages
by introducing the essences for their manufacture"
`[BUSH-1897 p.v (scan 9)]` — and warn against imitators selling "so called
Soluble Essences... nothing but mere tinctures" `[BUSH-1897 p.v (scan 9)]`.

## Services

- **Free superficial examination** of ærated waters; paid detailed analyses on a
  published scale — see [[water-quality-analysis]]
- **Free troubleshooting**: "we shall always be pleased when a Mineral Water
  Maker finds any difficulty in the manufacture of his drinks, through
  fermentation or other causes, if he will send us a sample for investigation in
  order that we may discover the cause of the difficulty."
  `[BUSH-1897 pp.vii–viii (scan 11–12)]`
- A separate pamphlet, *The Prospects of the Mineral Water Trade*, recommended
  to anyone starting in business `[BUSH-1897 pp.vi–vii (scan 10–11)]`

## Reading the book against its publisher

Every technical recommendation in the manual should be read as also a purchase
order. The **Suggested Order** for a beginner turning out 250 gross a week is
explicit about it `[BUSH-1897 p.104 (scan 108)]`: 20 lbs each of Soluble Essence
of Ginger and Lemon, 20 lbs Soluble Essence Ginger Ale, 1 cwt tartaric or citric
acid, 30 lbs French Cream, 1 gallon colouring, 7 lbs bicarbonate of soda, 2 lbs
Seltzer salts, 1 lb bicarbonate of potash, 7 lbs Bush's Preservative, 3 or 4
filtering bags, 28 lbs root ginger.

That said, the self-interest cuts both ways. Bush's warnings about lead, dirty
filters, and adulteration have no product attached to them and cost him
goodwill with his own customers — which is some evidence they were sincere. See
[[waterborne-contamination]].
