# United States Department of Agriculture

Issuing body of [[family-food-stockpile-1961]], *Home and Garden Bulletin No.
77*, August 1961, "prepared by the U.S. Department of Agriculture in cooperation
with the [[office-of-civil-and-defense-mobilization]]" `[FFS-1961 p.2]`.

Published from Washington, D.C., and sold by the Superintendent of Documents,
Government Printing Office, Washington 25, D.C., at 10 cents `[FFS-1961 p.2]`.

## Why Agriculture

The division of labour is visible in the text. USDA supplies the domestic-science
content — the eight food groups, servings and ounces per person per two weeks,
maximum storage periods by commodity, sample meal plans, freezer behaviour after
power loss `[FFS-1961 pp.4–9]`. OCDM supplies the threat model and the
two-week figure.

The water section reads as the join between the two: quantities and container
advice in the extension-service register, and a fallout warning box that
plainly is not.

## The Home and Garden Bulletin register

Worth noting for how the advice lands. This is the same series that told people
how to can tomatoes, and it addresses the reader the same way — a woman at a
kitchen cupboard, on the cover illustration, checking cans against a chart. The
nuclear content arrives inside a homemaking format:

> "Take into consideration the needs and preferences of family members. Familiar
> foods are likely to be more acceptable in times of stress." `[FFS-1961 p.3]`

The stockpile is to be rotated through ordinary meals — "As time approaches for
the replacement of particular food items, it is a good idea to use the food in
family meals" `[FFS-1961 p.7]` — so that the shelter supply and the pantry are
the same thing.
