# Baron de Bush, F.C.S., F.S.S.

Compiler of [[bush-recipes-aerated-mineral-waters-1897]] and chairman of
[[w-j-bush-and-co]].

The title page gives his honours at length `[BUSH-1897 title page (scan 5)]`:

> "BARON DE BUSH, F.C.S., F.S.S., &c., Knight of the Order of Leopold; Knight
> Officer of the Family Order of Saxe-Coburg; Knight of the Crown of Italy;
> President of Juries at various International Exhibitions. (Chairman.)"

F.C.S. — Fellow of the Chemical Society. F.S.S. — Fellow of the Statistical
Society. The exhibition jury appointments are itemised in the preface: London
1884, Antwerp 1885, Brussels 1888, Edinburgh 1890
`[BUSH-1897 pp.viii–ix (scan 12–13)]`.

## The compiler's claim

The recipes are presented as tested rather than collected:

> "To compile a Book of Recipes absolutely reliable in every particular, has not
> been by any means an easy task. Each single recipe has been thoroughly tested,
> and drinks made accordingly have been kept and subjected to all the changes to
> which ærated beverages are liable to be exposed." `[BUSH-1897 p.iii (scan 7)]`

## Voice

The preface is the most opinionated writing in the collection, and its author is
worth characterising: a trade insider of fifty years' standing who is
simultaneously selling essences and publicly attacking his industry's
standards.

On the trade's growth: "Sixty years ago there did not exist in the entire
country more than fifty Mineral Water Makers; to-day there are over six
thousand." `[BUSH-1897 p.iv (scan 8)]`

On what it used to be: the maker "was possessed of very little scientific
knowledge although there is still much to be desired in this respect", turning
out lemonade, ginger beer and soda water "in a very primitive fashion, and often
in dirty factories" `[BUSH-1897 p.iv (scan 8)]`.

On what it has become: new factories "fitted up on the most elaborate
principles", "absolutely no excuse now for dirty bottles, incomplete
aeration", and "some forty or fifty absolutely distinct flavours to select from"
`[BUSH-1897 p.iv (scan 8)]`.

On the price war: cheap sugar, short measure, inferior flavourings and mineral
acids mean "some beverages offered for sale are a scandal to the trade", and the
saving goes to the retailer, not the public
`[BUSH-1897 pp.vi–vii (scan 10)]`. Left unchecked, it "means part ruin to this
important and rising industry" `[BUSH-1897 p.vii (scan 11)]`.

On the limits of his own book — the honest note that opens the General
Instructions:

> "These General Instructions are not intended to teach the absolute novice the
> art of making ærated waters; no book can possibly do that. It can only be
> learnt by practical experience." `[BUSH-1897 p.11 (scan 15)]`

And, on flavour, the sentence that gives away what business he thinks he is in:
"a really good essence will cover a multiplicity of faults (but do not employ
this as an excuse for using inferior articles)" `[BUSH-1897 p.viii (scan 12)]`.
