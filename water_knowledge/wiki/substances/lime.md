# Lime

Appears in nearly every part of this collection: as an alkaline earth, as the
source of [[carbonic-acid]] in a gas generator, as the cause of hard water, as a
disinfectant, and as a bottled mineral water in its own right.

## The alkaline earth, 1809

Conversation XI: lime has "extensive Chemical Properties and Uses in the Arts"
`[COC-1809 contents (scan 9)]`. Among the four alkaline earths — barytes,
magnesia, lime, strontites `[COC-1809 scan 200]`.

Its defining behaviour, stated by contrast with magnesia: lime **attracts and
solidifies water** `[COC-1809 p.176 (scan 209)]`. Slaking, which appears in
Conversation IV as a standard example of heat released on mixing
`[COC-1809 contents (scan 7)]`. See [[caloric-theory]].

Lime has a **greater attraction for acids than magnesia**, and takes them from
it `[COC-1809 p.176 (scan 209)]`.

**Salts of lime:**
- *Carbonat of lime* — marble, chalk, shells, spars, calcareous stones; carbonic
  acid recoverable from them by sulphuric acid `[COC-1809 contents (scan 11)]`
- *Sulphat of lime* — "very frequently met with in nature, and constitutes the
  well known substance called gypsum, or plaister of Paris"
  `[COC-1809 p.201 (scan 235)]`
- *Muriat of lime* — the standing example of a **deliquescent** salt, absorbing
  water from the atmosphere until it dissolves in it `[COC-1809 p.201 (scan 235)]`

Lime's medicinal use, like magnesia's, is neutralising stomach acid
`[COC-1809 p.176 (scan 209)]`. And muriatic acid's principal combinations are
"with soda, lime, and magnesia" `[COC-1809 scan 254]`.

## In the 1897 factory

**As gas feedstock.** *Whiting* — powdered chalk — is the traditional charge for
a CO₂ generator: half-fill with whiting and water to "the consistency of thick
cream", then run in sulphuric acid `[BUSH-1897 p.18 (scan 22)]`. Chalk and marble
serve too `[BUSH-1897 p.17 (scan 21)]`. The drawback is the residue — Brunner,
Mond sell bicarbonate against it partly on "no lime mud to deal with"
`[BUSH-1897 scan 4]`. See [[artificial-carbonation]].

**As a disinfectant.** *Bisulphite of lime* is Bush's routine sanitiser: pipes
"occasionally... thoroughly cleaned and disinfected with a little bisulphite of
lime" `[BUSH-1897 p.14 (scan 18)]`, and in a mucoid-fermentation outbreak, "1
pint to a pailful of water" for plant and floor `[BUSH-1897 p.25 (scan 29)]`. See
[[preservatives-and-antiseptics]].

**As a product.** *Carrara Water*: "Mix thoroughly 1 gallon of freshly made Lime
Water with 10 gallons of cold water in a tank and bottle off."
`[BUSH-1897 p.88 (scan 92)]` The insistence on **freshly made** lime water is
sound — it absorbs atmospheric CO₂ and throws chalk on standing.

**As a defect.** The crystalline bottle deposit is calcium and magnesium
tartrates, "chiefly due... to the action of Tartaric Acid on the lime and other
salts in the water" `[BUSH-1897 p.26 (scan 30)]`. Lime in the supply water is the
problem; lime water in a bottle is the product. See
[[water-hardness-and-softening]].
