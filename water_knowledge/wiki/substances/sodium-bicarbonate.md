# Bicarbonate of soda

Two roles in the water trade, and Bush uses it for both: it is the **gas
generator feedstock** and it is the **active ingredient of soda water**.

## As gas feedstock

The cleaner and quicker of Bush's two routes to [[carbonic-acid]]: 1 cwt
bicarbonate, 17–20 gallons water, about 68 lbs sulphuric acid. Add the acid
"rather more cautiously than the Whiting", agitating gently until the end. Run
the spent liquor out hot so the sulphate of soda does not crystallise in the
generator. `[BUSH-1897 pp.18–19 (scan 22–23)]`

The by-product, Glauber's salt, "is sometimes saved, and either sold to alkali
makers or to the farmers in the neighbourhood as manure"
`[BUSH-1897 p.19 (scan 23)]`.

The Brunner, Mond & Co. advertisement bound into the book sells "Crescent Brand"
pure bicarbonate as "A PURE SUBSTITUTE FOR CHALK, WHITING, LIMESTONE, MARBLE,
&c." for exactly this purpose, claiming no plant alteration needed, three times
the gas per charge, great saving in acid, and "No Deposit in the residual
liquors, consequently no lime mud to deal with" `[BUSH-1897 scan 4]`.

## As the soda in soda water

> "For making Soda Water, the finest Bicarbonate of Soda should be used — not the
> commercial qualities, as these contain many impurities, and will not dissolve
> bright." `[BUSH-1897 p.89 (scan 93)]`

**Recipe:** dissolve 800 grains (about 1¾ oz avoirdupois) in a pint of hot water;
when cold, filter; add to 10 gallons of cold water in a tank; mix thoroughly and
bottle. Gives 5 grains per 10 oz bottle. For medicinal soda water, three times
that. `[BUSH-1897 p.89 (scan 93)]`

(The printed text reads "8 times" for the medicinal multiplier here, against
"three times" everywhere else in the section — see [[contradictions]].)

Selling soda water without it was prosecutable: "Many prosecutions with
convictions have lately taken place of mineral water makers selling Soda Water
without any Bicarbonate of Soda." `[BUSH-1897 p.87 (scan 91)]` See
[[mineral-waters]].

## In 1809

Marcet's Conversation X covers soda: resembling potash, "obtained from Sea Salt,
or from Marine Plants", with its carbonat among the compound salts
`[COC-1809 contents (scan 9)]`. Soda from the sea — the same alkali-from-brine
chemistry that Brunner, Mond would industrialise.

Soda water gets a short definition:

> "It is a kind of artificial Seltzer water, holding in solution, besides the
> gaseous acid, a particular saline substance, called soda, which imparts to the
> water certain medicinal qualities." `[COC-1809 p.122 (scan 152)]`

The American report is more expansive on its virtues, and on soda water as a
temperance drink — see [[mineral-waters]].

Soda was still an element in 1809 in the sense that nobody had taken it apart —
until [[humphry-davy]], whose isolation of sodium and potassium is the appended
Bakerian Lecture in this very edition `[COC-1809 title page (scan 3)]`.
