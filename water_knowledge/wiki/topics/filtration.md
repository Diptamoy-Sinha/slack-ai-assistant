# Filtration

Two quite different operations travel under this word in the collection:
**purifying the incoming water supply**, and **clarifying a finished syrup**.
Bush's 1897 manual does both, on different pages, with different equipment.

## Charcoal filtration of the water supply

The standing recommendation of the trade:

> "The water used should be exceptionally pure and clean; it is advisable to use
> as soft a water as possible. We recommend all Mineral Water Makers to filter
> the water through charcoal before use, particularly if there be any doubt as
> to its purity." `[BUSH-1897 pp.14–15 (scan 18–19)]`

[[charcoal]] is doing adsorption — removing taste, odour, and organic matter.
Bush does not claim it removes anything he would call a germ, and given that he
elsewhere blames fermentation on "living structures" `[BUSH-1897 p.24 (scan 28)]`,
the omission looks deliberate rather than ignorant.

"There is a great variety of filters on the market, nearly all of which are
capable of doing useful work." `[BUSH-1897 p.15 (scan 19)]`

### The maintenance warning

The most useful sentence in the section, and the one most likely to be ignored:

> "We must here caution all users of filters to see that they are maintained
> perfectly clean and the charcoal frequently replenished, as a dirty filter, or
> one that has been used for any length of time, **is worse than none at all**,
> and causes most disastrous results." `[BUSH-1897 p.15 (scan 19)]`

A saturated carbon bed stops adsorbing and starts shedding: it becomes a
substrate for exactly the growth the filter was installed to prevent. Bush
reaches the right operational conclusion — replace the medium on a schedule, not
on appearance — from a germ theory he only half states.

### When filtration is not enough

> "Should the water be very impure, or so highly charged with saline
> constituents as to be unfit for use, and no pure water obtainable, special
> advice must be sought on the subject. Fees for analysis are stated at the end
> of this work." `[BUSH-1897 p.15 (scan 19)]`

Charcoal does not touch dissolved salts. Bush knows the boundary of his method
and, characteristically, has a paid service on the other side of it — see
[[water-quality-analysis]].

## Filtration of syrups and finished waters

A separate craft, using **filtering bags**:

> "These Syrups are generally perfectly bright when made, but, for various
> reasons, they are sometimes cloudy after being mixed; when such is the case,
> pass them through a filtering bag. If they do not filter bright, a small
> quantity of finings must be sprinkled inside the bag, or better still,
> thoroughly mix with the Syrup, say in the proportion of 1 or 2 ozs. to the
> gallon, and then filter." `[BUSH-1897 p.29 (scan 33)]`

Paper pulp is an alternative to finings, and can be reused "once or twice, if
cleansed by thoroughly scalding it and well washing it in a stream of running
water until the water runs perfectly clean and tasteless" `[BUSH-1897 p.30 (scan 34)]`.

The same hygiene warning recurs, and it is the sharpest sentence in the manual:

> "Use different bags for each of the Syrups, and let them be frequently cleaned
> in boiling water. Never employ a bag if there is any dried-up Syrup on it; an
> old or dirty bag is the cause of much of the fermentation after æration."
> `[BUSH-1897 p.30 (scan 34)]`

The filter as vector of contamination, stated twice in a fifteen-page span. See
[[preservatives-and-antiseptics]].

Filtration also appears inside individual recipes — mineral-water salts are
dissolved in warm water and filtered before dilution `[BUSH-1897 p.88 (scan 92)]`
— and after adding oil-based flavourings, where "Filtration will, of course, be
necessary" `[BUSH-1897 p.64 (scan 68)]`.

## In 1809

Marcet has no filtration chapter. Where she needs to separate a dissolved
substance from water she reaches for [[distillation]] or evaporation, not a
filter — the distinction between *solution* and *mixture* being precisely that a
filter can catch the second and not the first. See [[water-as-solvent]].

One incidental passage has water "filtering through the crevices" of rock and
picking up earth in the process `[COC-1809 scan 198]` — filtration as a
geological process that *creates* [[mineral-waters]] rather than cleaning them.
The same operation, opposite sign.
