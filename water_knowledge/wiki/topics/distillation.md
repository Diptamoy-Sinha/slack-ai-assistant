# Distillation

One of the collection's named subjects. In these two sources it is rarely used
*on water* — it is a tool for separating everything else, and water is the
thing left behind.

## The apparatus

Marcet introduces the **alembic** with sublimation of sulphur, and it "is highly
useful in all kinds of distillations" `[COC-1809 scan 129]`.

The principle is stated most cleanly for spirits: to separate alcohol from water
"it is necessary to distil, or as the distillers say, to rectify"
`[COC-1809 p.263 (scan 295)]` — repeated distillation to raise purity.

## What gets distilled in 1809

- **Wine**, to analyse it: submitted to distillation it yields alcohol, "the
  product of the distillation of wine" `[COC-1809 scan 293]`. Distillation as an
  *analytical* method, not just a preparative one.
- **Rum**, from sugar; **brandy**, from fermented malt or from wine; **gin**,
  from juniper berries distilled with spirit `[COC-1809 scan 294–295]`
- **Essential oils and perfumes** — lavender distilled with alcohol; thyme, sage
  and other aromatics distilled after soaking in water `[COC-1809 scan 273–274]`.
  This is steam distillation, and it is the direct ancestor of the essence trade
  Bush was in.
- **Oxalic acid** from sorrel `[COC-1809 scan 280]`
- **Nitric acid** from nitrat of potash; **sulphurous acid** from sulphat of iron
  `[COC-1809 scan 227, 230]`
- **Gelatine**, decomposed by distillation `[COC-1809 scan 330]`
- **Naphtha** — the Davy appendix discusses "colourless and recently distilled"
  naphtha and "double distilled naphtha" as reference fluids
  `[COC-1809 scan 381, 386]`

Distillation is also how a salt is recovered from solution — evaporate the water
and "you would find the salt in the same state as before"
`[COC-1809 p.36 (scan 58)]`. See [[water-as-solvent]].

The physics is Conversation IV: latent heat, the phenomena attending "the
Melting of Ice and the Formation of Vapour", and the condensation of elastic
fluids `[COC-1809 contents (scan 7)]`. See [[evaporation-and-the-water-cycle]] and
[[caloric-theory]].

## Distilled water in 1897

Bush mentions it exactly once, as the fallback remedy for hard water:

> "the remedy for it would be to use soft or distilled water"
> `[BUSH-1897 p.26 (scan 30)]`

That is the whole of it. For a trade consuming water by the tank, distillation
was evidently not economic at the scale of a mineral water factory in 1897 —
[[filtration]] through charcoal was the routine treatment, and sourcing soft
water the preferred fix. See [[water-hardness-and-softening]].

Distillers appear in the book as *customers and suppliers*, not as water
treaters: the sugar merchant George Startin & Co. supplies "Mineral Water and
Cordial Makers, Brewers and Distillers" `[BUSH-1897 scan 3]`, and Potter & Moore
advertise as "Peppermint Growers & Distillers, Mitcham"
`[BUSH-1897 scan 4204]` — essential-oil distillation, exactly the operation
Marcet describes.

## Gap

Neither source treats **desalination**, and neither treats distillation as a
drinking-water supply technology. Given that the collection's stated subjects
include the extraction of magnesium from seawater, this is a hole other items in
the collection may fill. Filed in [[open-questions]].
