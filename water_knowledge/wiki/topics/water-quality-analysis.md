# Water quality, testing and analysis

The collection's headline subject. Across three sources the question "is this
water good?" gets three different answers, and the trajectory is not what you
would guess: it runs from *laboratory analysis* (1809) through *commercial
analysis* (1897) to *no analysis at all* (1961).

## 1809 — analysis as the enabling technology

Analysis is what makes the imitation trade possible. The claim is confident:

> "The accurate analysis of all the most important and most celebrated mineral
> waters has been accomplished, by men competent to the task; and we are thus
> informed not only concerning the nature, but the proportion of the ingredients
> which they contain." `[COC-1809 p.354 (scan 392)]`

Complete enough that "every species of mineral waters whatever can be prepared
by art". The [[ballston-spa]] and [[seltzer-water]] products of the New York
establishment "are prepared according to an accurate analysis"
`[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`, and the soda water "is made with the full
proportion of Soda directed by the dispensatories" `[COC-1809 p.356 (scan 394)]`.

Two standards of authority already in play, and they are not the same: the
**chemical analysis** of a natural spring, and the **pharmacopoeial formula**.
Bush inherits both, and the tension between them, in 1897.

Marcet's own analytical tools are qualitative and sensory. Carbonic acid in
water is identified by taste — "It has a distinct acid taste"
`[COC-1809 p.120 (scan 150)]`. [[sulphurated-hydrogen]] is identified by smell:
"it is evident, from the peculiar fetid smell of this gas, that it is a new
compound totally different from either of its constituents"
`[COC-1809 p.104 (scan 134)]`. The **eudiometer** appears for gas analysis
`[COC-1809 contents (scan 8)]`, and change of colour in vegetable blues serves as
an alkali test `[COC-1809 contents (scan 9)]`.

## 1897 — analysis with a price list

Bush's is the concrete document. He publishes a fee schedule
`[BUSH-1897 p.103 (scan 107)]`:

| Service | Fee |
|---|---|
| Superficial examination of ærated waters | free |
| **Water — general analysis:** ammonia, chlorine, nitrates, solid matter, "and our opinion as to fitness for drinking purposes" | £1 1s. |
| Complete and exhaustive analysis for brewers, manufacturers | £3 3s. to £5 5s. |
| Ærated waters — detection of lead and other impurities, general quality | £1 1s. per three kinds |
| Fermented beverages — determination of alcohol | 10s. 6d. |
| Essential oils, essences, colourings | free, except elaborate cases |
| Sugar, chemicals, everything else | £1 1s. upwards |

The four determinands in the guinea analysis are the standard late-Victorian
potable-water screen, and each is a **proxy**: ammonia and nitrates for sewage
contamination at different stages of oxidation, chlorine for saline or sewage
ingress, solid matter for total dissolved load. Nobody is counting organisms.
The chemist infers biological risk from chemical traces — and then supplies "our
opinion as to fitness for drinking purposes", a judgement, not a measurement.

**Sampling instructions**, and they are good ones:

> "The quantity required for Analysis is not less than ½ gallon, and should be
> most carefully collected in a perfectly clean stoppered Winchester quart, at
> the same time state if Water is taken from a well, stream, or a water
> company's main." `[BUSH-1897 p.103 (scan 107)]`

Provenance is part of the sample. A nitrate figure means something different
from a well than from a main.

The lead test is separately priced and separately reasoned: an ærated-water
analysis "should generally be accompanied with an Analysis of the water from
which the waters are made, except when only metallic impurities are to be sought
for" `[BUSH-1897 p.103 (scan 107)]`. Contamination is traced to source. See
[[waterborne-contamination]].

### In-house testing

Alongside the paid service, Bush teaches the maker to test without instruments:

- **Aeration quality** — the cork report and the white cloud
  `[BUSH-1897 p.12 (scan 16)]`; see [[artificial-carbonation]]
- **Fermentation** — "It can be roughly estimated by the sweetness of the
  liquor, as the more spirit produced the more sugar is consumed; if, therefore,
  the liquor has lost most of its sweetness, it should be analysed before
  sending out." `[BUSH-1897 p.22 (scan 26)]` A sensory pre-screen that triggers a
  paid assay, with a legal threshold behind it: over 8 per cent spirit and the
  drink attracts duty.
- **Deposit identification** — the crystalline "bee's wing" read as calcium and
  magnesium tartrates `[BUSH-1897 p.26 (scan 30)]`; see
  [[water-hardness-and-softening]]
- **Blue tinge in syrups** — traced to ultramarine or aniline blues used by sugar
  refiners, which in the presence of acid liberate "a small quantity of
  Sulphuretted Hydrogen, a gas possessing a most abominable odour"
  `[BUSH-1897 p.16 (scan 20)]`

The independent analyst also appears in the advertising: Thomas W. Pilcher,
Analytical Chemist, offering "ADVICE GIVEN / ANALYSIS MADE" to mineral water
manufacturers `[BUSH-1897 scan 108]` — Bush's competitor, bound into Bush's book.

## 1961 — analysis delegated, then abandoned

The pamphlet does not test water. It defers to an authority:

> "It is of the utmost importance that water stored for emergency use be clean.
> Any water that has been tested and approved by health authorities would be
> safe to store. If there is any question about the safety or cleanliness of the
> water you intend to store or if it has not been tested and approved by health
> authorities, it must be purified before it is stored." `[FFS-1961 p.11]`

Testing has become something municipal government does. The householder's job is
to know whether it has been done.

And in the scenario the pamphlet is actually written for, that infrastructure is
gone. What replaces analysis is **procedure with a built-in check**: dose to a
detectable chlorine residual and smell for it `[FFS-1961 p.12]`. It is a
one-parameter test — free chlorine present after 30 minutes — run on the nose,
requiring nothing. Bush's guinea buys four determinands and an expert opinion;
the pamphlet buys one determinand and gives it away.

Storage gets a standing check too: inspect containers every few months for leaks
and "check the water for cloudiness or other undesirable appearance or
undesirable taste. If undesirable appearances or tastes have developed, the
water should be changed." `[FFS-1961 p.12]` Turbidity, appearance, taste — the
same three senses Marcet's students used on the seltzer.

## The through-line

Every source arrives at the same place: **the trained nose and eye are the
instrument of first resort**, and the laboratory is where you go when they
report something wrong. 1809 tastes the acid; 1897 smells the sulphuretted
hydrogen and watches the cork; 1961 smells for residual chlorine. What changes
is what stands behind the senses — a professor, a paid analyst, or a health
department that may no longer exist.
