# Conversations on Chemistry (1809, third American edition)

**Full title:** *Conversations on Chemistry, in which the Elements of that
Science are Familiarly Explained and Illustrated by Experiments and Plates. To
Which Are Added, Some Late Discoveries on the Subject of the Fixed Alkalies by
H. Davy, Esq., of the Royal Society* `[COC-1809 title page (scan 3)]`

**Author:** [[jane-marcet]] — published anonymously, but attributed almost
immediately.
**Publisher:** From Sidney's Press, for Increase Cooke & Co., booksellers, New
Haven `[COC-1809 title page (scan 3)]`
**Date:** 1809. Third American edition; the first two were Philadelphia, 1806
and 1809.
**Digitised:** 422 scan pages, searchable PDF with an OCR text layer. Five pages
carry no text layer. Held by the Science History Institute (Othmer Library /
Roy G. Neville Historical Chemical Library bookplate visible at scan 5).

## Why it is in a water collection

Water is not a chapter here — it is the connective tissue. Marcet uses it as the
worked example for solution, for evaporation, for latent heat, and for chemical
composition, and the American editor bolted on a trade report about
manufacturing it. The book documents the moment when water stopped being an
element and became a compound you could take apart and put back together.

## Structure

Two volumes bound as one, in dialogue: **Mrs. B.** instructs **Caroline** and
**Emily**.

- **Vol. I — On Simple Bodies.** Conversations I–XI: general principles; light
  and heat; conduction, boiling and solution; specific and latent heat; oxygen
  and nitrogen; hydrogen; sulphur and phosphorus; carbone; metals; alkalies;
  earths.
- **Vol. II — On Compound Bodies.** Conversations XII–XXIII: attraction of
  composition; compound bodies; the sulphats and phosphats; the nitrats and
  carbonats; muriatic acids; vegetables; fermentation; vegetation; animal
  composition, economy, nutrition, respiration, and heat.

Three American additions follow the dialogues, and are the reason this edition
matters:

1. **Davy's Bakerian Lecture** on the decomposition of the fixed alkalies,
   pp. 338–351 `[COC-1809 contents (scan 13)]` — see [[humphry-davy]]
2. **Description and plate of the Pneumatic Cistern of Yale College**, p. 351
3. **An Account of Mineral Waters** (headed *Manufacture of Mineral Waters*),
   p. 356 (scan 392) — a trade report on the American artificial mineral water
   business, the single densest water passage in the book

Appendix: treatises on dyeing, tanning and currying.

## Page offset

Printed page + ~36 = scan page, through the main text. Verified at two points:
Account of Mineral Waters (printed 356 / scan 392) and Conversation VIII
(printed 121 / scan 151). The offset drifts slightly across the front matter.

## Cautions

- The chemistry is pre-Daltonian and runs on **caloric**, a weightless fluid of
  heat. Marcet's explanations of evaporation and solution are internally
  coherent and physically wrong; see [[caloric-theory]].
- Nomenclature is Lavoisian: *carbone* for carbon, *oxyd* for oxide, *muriatic*
  for hydrochloric, *sulphurated hydrogen* for hydrogen sulphide, *azote* /
  nitrogen interchangeably.
- OCR on the long *s* and on small caps is unreliable. The Account of Mineral
  Waters prints "April of this year, (1899)" where 1809 is meant — see
  [[contradictions]].
